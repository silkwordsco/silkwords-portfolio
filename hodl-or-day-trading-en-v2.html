HODL or day trading?

A practical guide for crypto beginners in Iran.

Audience

Trading sounded simple, until I tried to learn it.

Trading looked like a promising route to earn in a currency stronger than the rial. I knew the names of a few coins and assumed that, with some basic research, I could figure it out. To keep the risk low, I planned to start with only one million tomans.

Then I hit the information wall. Some resources were so detailed that they spent pages on Bitcoin's history; others were so technical that every sentence introduced another unfamiliar term. And social media made the picture worse, with claims that beginners should expect years of losses before becoming profitable.

The problem wasn't a lack of information. It was not knowing which path actually fit me.

Two paths: HODL vs. day trading

HODL means buying an asset and holding it for a longer period instead of reacting to short-term price movements. The term comes from a well-known misspelling of "hold." In simple terms: buy, hold, and focus on the longer horizon.

Day trading involves opening and closing positions over short time periods, often within the same day, to try to benefit from short-term price movements. It generally requires more active market monitoring and a stronger understanding of trading and risk-management concepts.

According to BabyPips, one of the well-known educational resources for trading, this need for technical analysis is exactly what makes day trading harder to learn as a beginner.

How to choose the right path

Before choosing either approach, use these four questions as a quick self-check.

01

How much time can you give the market?

If your schedule is limited, a longer-term approach may be easier to manage. Day trading requires consistent attention.

02

How do you react to losses?

If short-term swings strongly affect your decisions, frequent trading may add unnecessary pressure.

03

How comfortable are you with charts?

If technical analysis is new to you, learn the concepts first, then practise before risking real money.

04

What outcome are you looking for?

Long-term exposure fits HODLing; studying short-term moves is a different commitment.

For me, day trading could still have been an option, but only if I had stayed consistent with the learning process instead of rushing toward results.

Your first practical step

There are thousands of trading resources and strategies. You do not need all of them at once. First decide which path you are evaluating, then learn the fundamentals and practise before increasing your exposure.

Start small, or start with a demo. In a local exchange like Tabdeal, for example, the minimum wallet deposit is around 60,000 tomans. If even that feels like too much, a demo environment can help you practise execution and risk management without putting capital at stake.

Frequently asked questions

Is HODL safer than day trading?

Not necessarily safer, just different in risk. HODL is less exposed to short-term swings, but it still carries long-term market risk.

Do I have to choose only one approach?

No. Many people keep part of their capital in a long-term HODL position and practise day trading with a smaller portion.

How much money do I need to start?

It depends on the exchange; for example, the minimum deposit on Tabdeal is around 60,000 tomans. What matters more than the amount is starting with money you can afford to lose.

Which path fits someone with a full-time job?

Usually HODL, since it doesn't require constant market monitoring. Day trading needs more regular time and attention.

Final takeaway

The idea of earning more than the rial may sound attractive, but a sustainable approach starts with fit, not speed. The right path is the one that matches your goals, time, risk tolerance and willingness to learn.
